Chart Design
The charts use different visual forms, such as radar
charts, bar charts, line charts, and scatter plots, to
present data clearly and effectively. Consistent
colors are used to separate categories and highlight
comparisons, improving readability and contrast.

PRD
Problem
Students often do not know which study spaces on
campus best match their needs. Some places may
be too noisy, too crowded, or lack power outlets, but
this information is not easy to compare quickly. As
a result, students may waste time moving between
spaces or choose locations that do not support
their work well.
Goal
Create an interactive data visualization product
that helps students compare campus study spaces
based on student-reported experience. The product
should help users quickly find a place that fits their
study style, such as quiet solo work, group
collaboration, or long study sessions.
Target Audience and User Stories
Target Audience
1 .Sheridan students looking for a place to study on campus
2 .New students who do not know campus spaces well
3 .Students who have different needs depending on task, time, or environment
User Types
1 .The Focused Student: wants a quiet place with fewer distractions
2 .The Group Worker: wants a space that supports collaboration
3 .The Long-Stay Student: needs comfort, outlets, and reliable seating
Primary User Story
As a student, I want to compare study spaces on
campus, so that I can choose the best place for my
current task and comfort needs.

PRD
User Flow Chart
The user begins on the homepage, where a 3D interactive campus
map displays numbered markers for each study space. From here,
they can either filter spaces by learning mode (Solo, Group, Long
Study, Quick Stop) to highlight matching buildings, tap a map pin to
see a tooltip and open the detail panel, or scroll down to explore the
Analytics Dashboard with bar charts and scatter plots. The detail
panel presents comprehensive data — radar chart, hourly crowd
trend, amenities — and allows users to add up to 3 spaces to a
side-by-side comparison view or request a smart recommendation
based on personal preferences. All paths are non-linear; users can
freely switch between filtering, comparing, and browsing at any
point before choosing their ideal study space.

Knowledge Sharing 
My classmates’ sharing had a clear impact on my project design. Their
posts provided useful references for chart selection, interaction ideas,
survey distribution, and data handling. These resources helped me think
more carefully about how to collect data, how to update it through
Google Sheets, and how to choose the right visualizations for
comparison. They also reminded me to consider clarity, interpretation,
and context, not just aesthetics. Overall, peer sharing helped me improve
both the design and the data logic of my project.


--- PAGE 16 ---
When I started this project, I knew what I wanted to make, but I was not always sure how
to build every part of it efficiently. I used AI as a coding partner to help me move faster,
especially when I was building components, troubleshooting problems, and refining the
interface. I did not use AI to generate the whole project at once. Instead, I worked step by
step and used it as support throughout the process.
I broke the project into smaller parts, including the interactive map, data structure, filters,
comparison feature, and charts. For each part, I described what I wanted, reviewed the
result, tested it, and then asked for revisions when needed. This helped me realize that AI
works best when my instructions are specific. For example, telling it to use #003767 as the
primary color or clearly defining what should appear in a study-space comparison gave
much better results than vague prompts.
AI was also useful when bugs came up. When I ran into issues such as duplicate key
warnings in Recharts, I shared the error and used AI to help me understand what was
going wrong and what I could try next. Even then, I still had to decide what made sense for
my project, test the solution, and adjust the code myself.
The biggest thing I learned is that AI does not replace design thinking. I still made the
decisions about the layout, interactions, data presentation, and visual style. AI helped me
execute ideas faster, but the direction, judgment, and final outcome were still mine.
