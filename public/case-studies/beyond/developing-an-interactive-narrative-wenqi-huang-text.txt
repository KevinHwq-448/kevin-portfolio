Project Statement
 Project Statement
This project is an interactive narrative inspired by The Allegory of the Cave, set in the
everyday environment of the Sheridan Trafalgar campus. The story explores the
difference between what people see on the surface and what may be hidden
underneath. It asks participants to question the information, systems, and social
patterns that shape how they understand campus life.
At the beginning, participants experience a familiar and comfortable version of the
campus, where everything seems normal — this represents the “shadow world.” As
they move through different locations on campus and follow small clues, they start to
notice that some information does not fully match reality. These clues appear through
digital content, physical space, and layered narrative fragments.
The project focuses on themes of perception, conformity, and awareness. Participants
actively explore the story instead of simply reading it. Their choices influence how
much they question what they see and how far they are willing to go to discover hidden
truths. The experience encourages reflection and discovery, blending the boundary
between everyday campus life and narrative experience.

“Beyond the Shadows” is an interactive narrative inspired by Plato’s
Allegory of the Cave. The story focuses on how people understand reality and
how awareness can change their perspective. The experience takes place in a
familiar campus environment where everything first seems normal and stable.
However, small inconsistencies and strange signals begin to appear, suggesting
that what participants see may not be the full truth.
As participants explore the campus and follow clues, they can take different
paths. They may choose to stay comfortable and accept what they see, search
for deeper meaning, or face a more complex and unsettling reality. In this project,
awareness is not a single moment of discovery, but a gradual process that can
change how participants see themselves, others, and their place within the
campus environment.
While the project explores philosophical themes, it is designed to remain
engaging and experiential. Rather than presenting a fixed story, the narrative
unfolds through discovery, small anomalies, and participant-driven choices.
Moments of curiosity, uncertainty, and gradual realization create a sense of
intrigue, encouraging participants to actively explore and interpret the
experience.

The Participant
The participant takes the role of a student at the Sheridan Trafalgar campus. At first, they
experience the campus as ordinary and predictable. However, they begin to notice small
irregularities — familiar spaces feel slightly altered, messages appear incomplete, and
information seems inconsistent. Driven by curiosity, the participant starts to question what
they perceive and must decide whether to remain comfortable or pursue deeper
understanding.
Narrative function
Represents curiosity, interpretation, and the journey from comfort toward awareness.

What does your character think about themselves?
They see themselves as a normal student living an ordinary routine, but they often feel there

What does your character think about their world?
They believe the world usually makes sense, but sometimes feels strangely repetitive and

What does your character think about others?
They see others as people following their own routines, often unaware of what is happening

are the only one seeing beyond the surface.

The Collective (Shadow Community)
The collective represents the majority of students who accept the surface reality. They
reinforce normalcy and familiarity, often attributing anomalies to coincidence. Their
presence creates social comfort but also brings a subtle pressure, compelling conformity and
avoiding questioning.
Sometimes, collective members repeat the same phrases, ignore contradictions, or react
defensively when someone questions shared beliefs—suggesting that some subconscious
influence may exist beneath the surface.
Narrative Function
Represents conformity, social stability, and resistance to change. Students are all doing
their jobs.

The Messenger (The One Who Saw)
The Messenger appears only through fragments—handwritten notes, altered posters, voice
recordings, or hidden digital traces. This figure has already "seen beyond the shadows" and
attempts to guide the participant. "I'll place QR codes or other instructions at a location
within the school, depending on the story's context, to guide the character."
However, the Messenger never provides full answers. Their messages are incomplete,
sometimes cryptic, and occasionally contradictory, forcing the participant to interpret
meaning rather than blindly follow instructions.
Narrative function: Represents awakening, uncertainty, and the possibility of seeing beyond
surface reality.

The initial inspiration comes from Plato’s Allegory of the Cave. I am interested in how
people can mistake perception for reality, and how awareness can change the way we
understand familiar environments. This idea will be explored within the everyday setting of
the Sheridan Trafalgar campus.
At this stage, the narrative is still developing. Rather than defining a fixed storyline, the
project focuses on a general direction: a familiar campus environment where small
inconsistencies begin to appear over time. These subtle changes may suggest that what
seems normal is not entirely complete or stable.
The project will likely involve a participant gradually noticing irregularities across a few
campus spaces, leading to a shift in perception. Instead of a fully defined plot, the current
goal is to explore atmosphere, discovery, and how meaning can emerge through observation
and interpretation.
This early idea is also influenced by works such as Inside, The Matrix, and Superliminal,
which use subtle storytelling and perception-based interaction rather than direct
explanation. These references inform the tone and direction of the project as it continues to
develop.

Brainstorm
Brainstorm
Brainstorm
Visually
I want the story to happen inside the school, using small changes in the
environment. At first everything looks normal, like a regular day on campus.
But slowly, small strange details appear, such as repeated signs, lights
changing, or spaces feeling a bit different. These small changes make
people start to feel something is not right.
As the experience continues, the space feels more confusing and less
stable. Things might repeat, look slightly wrong, or feel unfamiliar even
though they are in the same place. The goal is to slowly create curiosity,
not big dramatic changes.
Textually
The story will use short pieces of text instead of long explanations. People
might see small notes, strange messages, or altered information around
the space. These texts do not clearly explain what is happening, but
suggest that there is something hidden.
As more text appears, people start to understand the situation by
themselves. The information might sometimes feel unclear or even
conflicting, showing that perception is not always reliable.

Using fewer locations makes the project easier to manage while still
keeping the experience meaningful.

Story Structure — Beyond the Shadows
Step 1 — Opening
A student is walking on Sheridan campus during a normal day. Everything
looks the same as usual — people studying, walking, talking. But the
student starts noticing small strange details, like repeated signs or
messages that feel slightly wrong.
Nothing dramatic happens, but the space feels a little off. The story
begins with curiosity.
Step 2 — The Challenge
The student becomes more aware of these strange details and wants to
understand what is happening. They are not sure if it is real or just
coincidence, but they feel pulled to keep noticing and exploring.
The challenge is deciding whether to ignore the strange feeling or
investigate further.
Step 3 — Direction
The student chooses to pay more attention. As they move through the
campus, the environment feels less stable. Some things repeat, some
information changes, and reality feels uncertain.
The story moves toward questioning what is real.
Step 4 — Conflict
The student tries to make sense of what they see, but things
don’t fully match. Others around them seem normal and
unaware, which makes the student feel isolated and unsure.
The more they notice, the harder it becomes to trust what is
real.
Step 5 — Resolution
The student does not find a clear answer, but realizes that
reality may not be fixed. Their understanding changes, even
though the environment stays the same.
The change happens inside the student, not outside.
Step 6 — Closing / Interaction
The student continues walking through campus, but now sees
it differently. The story does not end with a solution, but with
awareness.
Participants experience this journey through observation,
movement, and small discoveries.

Platforms
Platforms
Platforms
The project uses both physical and digital platforms to create a puzzle-based exploratory
narrative across the Sheridan Trafalgar campus.
Physical Platform — Campus Space
The physical campus acts as the main narrative environment. Participants move through
familiar locations such as hallways, study areas, and shared spaces. These places appear
normal at first, but subtle inconsistencies and hidden clues encourage participants to
observe more carefully and question what they see.
Physical exploration represents the surface reality — a familiar world that slowly feels
less stable as participants progress.
Digital Platform — Interactive Clues
Digital elements provide hidden layers of the story. Participants may encounter coded
messages, altered information, or small interactive puzzles through screens, phones, or
projected media. These clues help reveal deeper meaning behind the physical environment.
Digital interaction represents the search for truth — where participants begin connecting
fragments and uncovering hidden patterns.

Risk Assessment
One possible risk is that the narrative becomes too complex or unclear, making it difficult
for participants to understand the experience. To reduce this, the project focuses on a
simple core idea and gradual progression rather than a complicated storyline.
Another risk is technical limitation, such as time, tools, or scope becoming too large. To
manage this, the project uses simple environments, minimal animation, and limited
locations to keep development realistic and achievable.

Skills Assessment
At the start of this project, I noticed my main strengths are concept development and
narrative thinking. I am good at generating ideas and shaping themes. When working with
The Allegory of the Cave, I quickly connected it to perception and reality on campus,
which helped form the core direction of the project. I also feel confident in visual and
atmospheric thinking, especially when imagining how participants experience the
narrative.
However, I also recognized some weaknesses. Sometimes my ideas are too broad, and I
needed time to simplify the concept and focus on what really matters. I am also less
confident in structured planning and organization, and turning ideas into clear, step-by-
step execution was challenging at first.
During the project, I improved by refining my ideas, reducing complexity, and focusing on
clarity. This process helped me better understand how to balance creativity with
structure, and I hope to continue improving my planning and execution skills in future
projects.

AI Usage Reflection
AI Usage Reflection
AI Usage Reflection
AI was used as a support tool during this project to help with
brainstorming, organizing ideas, and improving clarity in writing. It assisted
in structuring the narrative, refining concepts, and keeping the proposal
focused.
However, all creative decisions, themes, and final direction were developed
personally. AI supported the process but did not replace original thinking or
design.
AI Use Plan for Project 2 and Project 3
For Project 2 and Project 3, AI will continue to be used as a support tool rather than a
replacement for creative thinking. AI may assist with brainstorming interaction ideas,
organizing narrative structure, and refining clarity in writing and presentation.
In Project 2, AI may help explore possible puzzle flows, user experience paths, and ways
to improve interaction clarity. It may also support generating variations of visual or
narrative ideas during early prototyping.
In Project 3, AI may assist with refining storytelling, improving user experience, and
testing different narrative structures. AI may also help identify gaps, simplify complex
ideas, and improve overall communication of the project.
However, all core concepts, creative direction, and final design decisions will remain
personally developed. AI will be used as a tool to support efficiency, exploration, and
reflection throughout the design process.
